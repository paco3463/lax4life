The story of lacrosse

The oldest team sport in North America, from the Creator's Game to Saturday mornings in Miami.

Every time a Stone Crab picks up a stick, they're playing a game that is centuries older than the United States. Here's how it travelled from the nations who created it to the fields of South Florida.

The Creator's Game

Before it had a French name, the game belonged to the nations of eastern North America.

  1. AncientTime immemorial

    A game with a purpose

    Indigenous nations played with wooden sticks and a ball long before Europeans arrived. Many Haudenosaunee people call it the Creator's Game: it was played for the Creator's pleasure, to pray together, and to bind communities. Medicine men served as coaches, preparing players and their sticks with ritual. A single game could involve 100 to 1,000 players from rival villages.

  2. NamesNation by nation

    Every nation had its own word for it

    • DehontsigwaehsOnondaga: "they bump hips"
    • Tewa:arátonMohawk
    • Baaga'adoweOjibwe: "bump hips"
    • Kapucha toliChoctaw: "little brother of war"

A name and a rulebook

Europeans wrote it down, then a Montreal dentist turned it into a codified sport.

  1. 1637

    The first written account

    Jesuit missionary Jean de Brébeuf watches Huron-Wendat players and writes the first known description of the game, which the French called crosse. The name stuck: la crosse.

  2. 1763

    The game that took a fort

    During Pontiac's War, Ojibwe players stage a game outside British Fort Michilimackinac in present-day Michigan, work the play toward the open gates, then rush in and take the fort.

  3. 1834

    Montreal takes notice

    Mohawk players from Caughnawaga (today Kahnawake) demonstrate the game in Montreal. Newspapers cover it, and the city's English-speaking clubs start learning from their Mohawk neighbours.

  4. 1856–67

    Clubs and written rules

    The Montreal Lacrosse Club forms in 1856. In 1867, Dr. William George Beers, who learned the game from the Mohawks of Kahnawake, writes down the first formal rules and sets teams at 12 players.

Across America and the world

Colleges, women's teams, the Olympics, and a Native nation that still competes on its own passports.

  1. 1877

    The first U.S. college team

    New York University becomes the first American college to field a lacrosse team.

  2. 1890

    Women pick up the stick

    Students at St. Leonard's School in Scotland play the first women's game. In 1926, Rosabelle Sinclair starts the first U.S. women's program at the Bryn Mawr School in Baltimore.

  3. 1904 + 1908

    An Olympic sport

    Lacrosse is a medal sport at the St. Louis and London Olympics. At the 1932 Los Angeles Games it returns as an exhibition, with more than 80,000 fans in the Coliseum.

  4. 1971

    NCAA champions

    Cornell wins the first NCAA men's championship. The first NCAA women's title follows in 1982, won by UMass.

  5. 1990

    The Creator's Game returns to the world stage

    The Iroquois Nationals, now the Haudenosaunee Nationals, play their first men's world championship and finish fifth. They compete as a sovereign nation, separate from the U.S. and Canada, and travel on Haudenosaunee passports.

  6. 1994

    Canada makes it official

    The National Sports of Canada Act names lacrosse Canada's national summer sport, alongside hockey in winter.

  7. 2023

    Back to the Olympics

    Lacrosse is voted onto the program for the 2028 Los Angeles Olympics, more than a century after its last medal event. Today the game is played in 97 countries, with more than 900 NCAA programs in the U.S.

South Florida catches on

In a region built on football, baseball and soccer, Florida lacrosse has grown as fast as almost anywhere in the country over the past two decades.

  1. 2008

    A South Florida state champion

    Saint Andrew's School in Boca Raton wins the first FHSAA boys lacrosse state championship. South Florida schools like St. Thomas Aquinas in Fort Lauderdale join the list of champions in the years that follow.

  2. 2010

    College lacrosse comes to Florida

    Jacksonville plays the first Division I women's game in the state on February 6, and the Florida Gators play their first game two weeks later. The same year, the Stone Crabs start playing in Miami.

  3. 2021

    The Americas meet in Florida

    The PALA Sixes Cup in Auburndale brings national teams from Puerto Rico, Colombia, Peru, Panama, Argentina and the U.S. Virgin Islands to Florida, alongside the Iroquois Nationals.

Miami makes it its own

Lacrosse is a young sport across most of Latin America. In Miami, Latin families are writing its next chapter.

  1. 1961Havana to Miami

    A Cuban school fields a lacrosse team

    Belen Jesuit was founded in Havana in 1854 and re-founded in Miami in 1961 after Cuba's new government seized the school. Today its varsity lacrosse team plays in Miami and has won three district championships in a row.

  2. 2015

    Puerto Rico Lacrosse forms

    Puerto Rico's national program is founded and builds teams from players across the mainland, including Florida, while working to bring the game into schools on the island.

  3. 2021

    A Miami club that looks like Miami

    The Miami Sol girls' club grows to about 140 players on six teams, with many Hispanic players: Cuban American, Colombian, Venezuelan and Peruvian. Players like Alissa Gonzalez-Mir came over from soccer, which she says helped her pick up the game.

  4. Today

    Bienvenidos al juego

    Miami's fields now bring together players whose families trace back to Cuba, Colombia, Venezuela, Peru and Puerto Rico, all playing a game that began with North America's first nations. The Stone Crabs have been part of that story since 2010, and every season adds new families to it.

“We have a huge opportunity to introduce our sport to so many different people.”

Jenna Martino-DiFede, founder of the Miami Sol, in USA Lacrosse Magazine, 2021

Questions

Can't find your answer? Send us a message and a coach will get back to you within a day.

Does my child need lacrosse experience?
No. Every program groups players by age and level, and our Youth and Junior groups are built for first-time players. Loaner sticks are available.
How are age groups decided?
By the player's age on August 31, 2026 for all 2026–27 seasons. A player who is 11 on that date plays U12. Use the age finder to check.
Do you have girls programs?
Yes. Girls academy and girls teams run in every season, coached under girls' rules with girls' equipment.
What happens when it rains or there's lightning?
Practices move or pause for lightning. We clear the field at the first strike and wait 30 minutes after the last one. Cancellations go out by text and email at least an hour before start time.
What is the refund policy?
Full refund up to 7 days before the season starts. After that we credit missed sessions toward a future season. Registration and cancellation for Pinecrest programs are handled by Pinecrest Parks & Recreation at 305-284-0900.
Do you offer sibling or financial assistance?
Siblings get 10% off each additional player. If cost is a barrier, email us. We don't want any kid to miss out.
